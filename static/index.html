      Six scams to recognise

      Most scams in Nigeria follow one of these patterns. Know them and you can spot them quickly.

      “Hi Mum, new number” emergencies

      Looks like: a message from “your child” on an unknown number, asking for money because their phone is lost or damaged.

      Do: call the old number or another relative before sending anything.

      Jobs that charge fees

      Looks like: a job or scholarship offer that asks for registration, form or training money.

      Do: walk away. Genuine employers do not charge to hire you.

      OTP and verification codes

      Looks like: “I sent my code to your number by mistake, please send it back.”

      Do: never share a code. It gives the sender access to your account.

      Fake supplier invoices

      Looks like: a supplier suddenly changes their bank account and asks you to pay today.

      Do: confirm by calling a number you already have, not the one on the invoice.

      Investments that double your money

      Looks like: “Invest ₦50,000 and get ₦200,000 in a week, guaranteed.”

      Do: no real investment guarantees profit. Avoid chat-group schemes.

      Lookalike links

      Looks like: a bank or network name with extra hyphens or a strange ending, like .xyz or .top.

      Do: type the official address yourself or use the official app.

      How PhishGuard NG works

      Built by Team CyberShield NG for Nascomsoft Hackathon 2.0.

      1. Paste

        Copy the message you received, and the link if there is one.

      2. We analyse

        Rules tuned to Nigerian scams look for red flags. A small machine-learning model, running on the server with no paid API, adds a second opinion.

      3. You decide

        You get a risk level, the reasons behind it, and clear next steps.

      PhishGuard NG can miss scams and can sometimes flag real messages. Treat it as a second pair of eyes, not a guarantee.
